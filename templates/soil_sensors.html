Agrivoltaic Soil Sensors: A Complete Guide

Selecting optimal sensors for agrivoltaic experiments and monitoring microclimate conditions

What is Agrivoltaics?

🔆
+
🌾

Agrivoltaics combines solar panels with agriculture on the same land, maximizing land use efficiency. Solar panels create partial shading, altering the microclimate beneath them.

⚠️ Critical: Monitoring soil conditions is essential due to altered microclimate from panel shading, including changes in temperature, moisture patterns, and nutrient cycling.

Critical Soil Parameters for Agrivoltaic Monitoring

💧

Soil Moisture/Humidity

Critical

Measures water content in soil - essential for irrigation management under variable shading

🌡️

Soil Temperature

Critical

Tracks thermal changes from panel shading affecting plant root development

⚡

Electrical Conductivity (EC)

Critical

Indicates salinity, dissolved salts, and overall soil health

🌱

NPK Nutrients

Critical

Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium - essential nutrients affected by altered conditions

🧪

Soil pH

Moderate

Controls nutrient availability - important but secondary priority

Key Insights

💧

Moisture = Humidity

In soil sensors, "moisture" and "humidity" are used interchangeably - both measure soil water content, not air humidity

🧪

pH: Important but Secondary

Research shows pH changes minimally in most agrivoltaic systems - can be added later if needed

⚡

EC as pH Proxy

Electrical conductivity can serve as a proxy indicator for pH issues and soil health
